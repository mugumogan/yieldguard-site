Hospital price transparency

What counts as a shoppable service

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

A shoppable service is a service a healthcare consumer can schedule in advance. That is the definition in 45 CFR 180.20. It is not a synonym for every item in a chargemaster, and it is not limited to services that are always scheduled ahead of time.

CMS gives examples such as joint replacements, physical therapy, imaging, laboratory services and outpatient visits. Hospital administration of medication can qualify when it can be scheduled in advance, such as a planned infusion. A service sometimes delivered in an emergency can still be shoppable in another circumstance. The question is whether advance scheduling is possible, not whether it happens on every occasion.

The primary service is only part of the consumer-facing picture. The rule defines ancillary services as items or services the hospital customarily provides as part of, or in conjunction with, the shoppable primary service. Standard charges for those ancillary services belong with the shoppable service as applicable. If the hospital has established a package charge, it displays that package rather than inventing individual component charges.

A useful internal check is to have scheduling staff and finance staff review the proposed service list together. Scheduling confirms that a consumer can book the service in advance; finance confirms what the hospital actually provides and charges. This is a recommended review workflow, not an extra certification required by the rule.
