Hospital price transparency

How to structure an internal compliance review

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

There is no universal first check that proves a hospital meets every price transparency requirement. CMS's FAQ says a comprehensive review examines the machine-readable disclosure against 45 CFR 180.50 and the consumer-friendly disclosure against 45 CFR 180.60. An internal review should cover both, while being clear that it is not CMS's assessment.

CMS's enforcement-policy discussion describes prioritizing cases based on apparent degree of noncompliance, including allegations that no MRF has been published. That policy does not establish a fixed order used by every auditor or make later checks optional. Accessibility is a sensible starting point for internal work because an inaccessible disclosure cannot be inspected normally.

A recommended sequence is to locate the intended disclosure, test ordinary public access, check format and required fields, examine data completeness and relationships, and review the consumer-friendly route separately. Record the requirement tested and the evidence for each result. A successful download or parser run answers only the question it actually tested.

If the review supports a corrective action, identify the source change, responsible owner and repeatable retest. Mark unknown cases for review rather than assigning a pass based on an assumption. This is a proposed internal workflow, not a statement about what CMS or third-party auditors always check first.
