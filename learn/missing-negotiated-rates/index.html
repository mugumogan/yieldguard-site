Missing negotiated rates: why it happens

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

The payer-specific negotiated charge is one of the five standard charges every hospital machine-readable file must include for each item or service. When it is missing from a file, the CMS FAQ points to three causes: the contract stores the rate somewhere the file-building process never looked, the rate is expressed as a percentage or algorithm and the file builder treated it as unrepresentable, or the charge genuinely was never established for that item.

The first two are process failures. CMS has noted that the negotiated charge often lives outside the chargemaster, in rate tables or rate sheets inside payer contracts. A file built only from the chargemaster will miss them. A charge expressed as a percentage or algorithm is not exempt from the file: the hospital encodes what can be expressed as a dollar amount and, for the rest, the information needed to derive a dollar amount, including the specific fee schedule or components the percentage or formula references.

The June 2026 CMS FAQ distinguishes a charge that was not established from an export omission. Hospitals must include each required data element and leave blanks where no applicable standard charge information exists to encode. If no standard charge at all has been established for an item or service, CMS says to include only items/services for which one or more standard charges have been established. Do not fabricate a charge or a universal status marker. Follow the current dictionary for the actual field and case.

The attestation makes this personal. The named senior official affirms the file is true, accurate and complete as of its date. A systematic gap in negotiated rates is exactly the kind of incompleteness a reviewer can find by sampling a handful of common services against the payer list.
