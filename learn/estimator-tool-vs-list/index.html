Hospital price transparency

Price estimator tool vs published list

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

A hospital can satisfy the consumer-friendly disclosure requirement with either the published shoppable-services list or a qualifying internet-based price estimator. It cannot use either route to replace the comprehensive machine-readable file. The two duties remain separate under 45 CFR 180.40.

The published-list route displays the required standard charges and other data elements. The estimator route instead must let a consumer obtain, when using the tool, an estimate of the amount that individual would be obligated to pay the hospital. CMS clarifies that this is a personalized single out-of-pocket dollar amount, not merely a range across commercial payers.

A qualifying estimator covers the CMS-specified services the hospital provides and the additional hospital-selected services needed for the required scope. It must be prominent on the hospital website, free to use, and accessible without registration or an account or password. The CMS FAQ explicitly allows collection of insurance information or other personal identifying information needed to generate a real-time personalized estimate. The published list's prohibition on requiring PII must not be copied onto the estimator alternative.

CMS encourages useful estimator disclaimers but does not require them under these HPT provisions. Disclaimers can explain limitations and suggest confirming insurance responsibilities, but they do not replace the personalized estimate or cure missing functionality. Recommended practice is to test both an insured and an uninsured path, document what amount the tool returns, and confirm that a consumer can finish without creating an account.
