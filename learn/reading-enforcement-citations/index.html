Reading the requirements cited in CMS notices

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

A CMS enforcement notice should be read as a set of case-specific findings tied to requirements, not as a ranking of the industry's most common mistakes. The enforcement regulation covers the duties to make charges public, the machine-readable disclosure, and the consumer-friendly disclosure. A useful review begins with the exact provision cited.

For each finding, distinguish the requirement from the observation supporting it. A missing public file, a failure to meet the prescribed form and manner, and an incomplete field are different issues. The corrective action should answer the actual deficiency rather than merely make a validator stop reporting an error.

A CMP notice may identify both the underlying disclosure violations and a failure to respond to a CAP request or comply with CAP requirements. It can also identify an effective violation date and explain continuing penalties. Do not reduce the notice to a list of technical errors and lose the response obligations.

Recommended practice is to use a requirement-to-evidence table: cited provision, CMS finding, affected disclosure, correction owner, retest evidence and unresolved question. No frequency ranking is claimed here. Establishing what notices cite most would require a defined notice corpus, period and counting method, including treatment of repeat notices for the same case.
