Hospital price transparency

The 70 CMS-specified shoppable services

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

The 70 CMS-specified shoppable services are the starting set, not the entire consumer-friendly display. A hospital must include as many of those services as it provides, then add hospital-selected services to reach at least 300 in total. If it provides fewer than 300 shoppable services, it lists as many as it provides.

The official list is reproduced in the CMS guide "10 Steps to Making Public Standard Charges for Shoppable Services", under Step 2, and in Table 3 of the original final rule. It covers evaluation and management, laboratory and pathology, radiology, and medicine and surgery services. Use the linked CMS list as the reference rather than a vendor summary.

The CMS guide labels its listed primary codes as 2020 CPT, HCPCS or DRG codes. That date matters: the guide permits appropriate code substitutions and crosswalks to display the hospital's established standard charges. Treat it as the specified service list with its original code references, not as a claim that every printed code remains the current billing code for every use.

A missing service and a missing price are different. If the hospital does not provide a CMS-specified service, it must clearly indicate that fact, using "N/A" or another appropriate indicator. It must not silently drop the service and leave readers guessing. Recommended practice is to retain a mapping showing the specified service, the hospital's corresponding service/code, whether it is provided, and where it appears in the public display.
