03 · CFO + managed care

For genuinely comparable services, are our published rates higher or lower than nearby hospitals?

There is no single hospital-wide price premium to negotiate.

The cash comparison points one way: the median of Site B/A matched posted cash ratios is 0.8378, or 16.2% lower at B. The inpatient estimated-amount comparison is mixed: across 105 exact-label DRG/payer-plan groups, Site B is higher in 54, lower in 48 and equal in three; the median paired B/A index is 100.4. Those are different price types and service populations, so keep them separate. The decision is to review specific service and payer-plan groups, starting with the high-volume DRGs identified here, instead of asking for one blanket cross-hospital uplift.

09 · Managed care + CFO

Which rates are comparable enough to use in a negotiation, and which should we exclude?

Do not median-collapse ambiguous service keys. Compare the clean lines and quarantine the rest.

In the two-file cash test, after excluding drug-information items and modifiers at both item and charge level, 1,711 code/type/setting/facility-class keys are shared. Of these, 643 still carry multiple distinct cash figures in at least one file. Exclude those groups from a one-price comparison. Of the remaining 1,068 single-price keys, 10 have different description sets. The 1,058 positive exact-description matches support the posted-line comparison shown here. For inpatient estimated amounts, separately require the same MS-DRG, payer-plan label, setting, billing class, methodology, algorithm text and percentage where present. That yields 105 matched groups across 49 DRGs.

12 · Compliance + contracting

Did we publish all relevant payer plans and contracted services, or just a plausible-looking subset?

One health system does not produce one payer-plan inventory.

The two retained files declare 29 and 38 distinct payer-plan labels. Twenty-eight labels appear in both, leaving one label unique to Site A and ten unique to Site B. That is a concrete reconciliation list for the contract owner, rather than a vague completeness question. Compare the declared labels and service coverage against the active contract inventory; do not automatically add the other hospital's labels, because the hospitals can have different contracts. File counts identify exactly what needs explaining, while the contract inventory determines whether an omission exists.

14 · CFO + patient financial services

Are our self-pay cash prices competitive for comparable local services?

The same 45% cash discount still leaves a 16.2% posted-price gap.

Both hospitals price every matched cash key at 55% of gross, within cent rounding: the same 45% discount. Yet across 1,058 positive, exact-description, single-price matches, Site B is lower on 734 items, equal on 64 and higher on 260. The median of the paired B/A ratios is 0.8378, or 16.2% lower at B. The gap sits in the underlying gross schedules, not the percentage discount. Start Site A's review with the high-gap gross-price lines, while retaining the 260 cases where B is higher. Removing 12 generic-description keys leaves 1,046 matches and a 16.3% median gap. The earlier four-file pilot also showed why discount labels alone mislead: three files' cash columns simply equaled gross.

15 · CFO + managed care

Which high-volume services deserve a closer reimbursement review first?

Start inpatient reimbursement review with sepsis, heart failure and pneumonia.

A new join to CMS's 2024 hospital-by-DRG data puts a real volume order behind that choice. The two hospitals have 693 discharges in the published Original Medicare fee-for-service rows. Severe sepsis with MCC (DRG 871) contributes 163, heart failure/shock with MCC (291) contributes 127, and simple pneumonia/pleurisy with MCC (193) contributes 56. Together that is 346 of 693, or 49.9%. Those three DRGs also have six, three and two matched payer-plan estimated-amount groups respectively. Begin there. Use the Medicare counts to rank service attention, not as commercial or Medicare Advantage contract volumes.

16 · Managed care + CFO

Are particular payer contracts below comparable local market rates?

Target the payer-plan group, not an across-the-board hospital premium.

Across 105 matched inpatient DRG/payer-plan groups, the median Site B/A published estimated-amount index is 100.4 with Site A set to 100. Yet the largest matched payer-plan group, covering 24 DRGs, has a median index of 122.8; Site B is higher on 21 of those 24. Another group covering seven DRGs has a median index of 86.8. That is a specific contract-review queue and a reason to reject a blanket rate-gap story. These fields are algorithm-based estimated allowed amounts, not the contract's negotiated dollar schedule. Use the largest recurring differences to select contract clauses and actual remittances for review, not to book recovery revenue.

17 · CFO + managed care

What would a proposed contract rate change do to our revenue?

A 5% rate increase breaks even after a 4.76% loss of eligible volume.

The scenario equation is (1 + rate change) x (1 + eligible volume change) - 1. On unchanged eligible volume, a 5% increase applied to 20% of revenue raises total revenue by 1.0%; a 10% increase applied to the same share raises it by 2.0%. The corresponding eligible-volume break-even losses are 4.76% and 9.09%. This is useful negotiating math without pretending that a posted-price gap is collectible revenue. The scenario table makes those assumptions explicit. A hospital forecast would replace the assumed share and volume with its actual contract-covered cases and payment provisions.

18 · Managed care + compliance

Why do the hospital's and payer's published rates disagree?

Resolve scope and effective date before calling a hospital/payer difference a rate dispute.

The reconciliation key must include provider identity, payer product/network, code version, modifiers, place of service, professional versus facility scope, reimbursement method and effective date. Match that key first, then compare the amounts and the applicable contract provision. An insurer's professional fee line and a hospital facility line are not competing answers to the same question. The retained evidence contains no paired payer-side file, so it establishes no actual hospital-versus-payer discrepancy. The concrete control is an exception table keyed to those fields, not an average of unmatched prices.
