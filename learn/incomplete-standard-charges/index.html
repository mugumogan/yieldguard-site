Hospital price transparency

Incomplete standard charge fields

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

The rule names five standard charges for every item or service: the gross charge, the discounted cash price, the payer-specific negotiated charge, and the de-identified minimum and maximum negotiated charges. A file can carry thousands of rows and still be incomplete if any of the five is systematically absent.

Each field fails in its own way. Gross charges are usually present because they come straight from the chargemaster. Discounted cash prices get skipped when a hospital believes it has none; the FAQ still requires the hospital to reflect that status in the display rather than omit the element. Payer-specific negotiated charges go missing for the reasons covered in the missing-rates page. The de-identified minimum and maximum are frequently empty because the file builder never computed them across plans.

Encoding details matter as much as presence. A negotiated charge of zero dollars is a real negotiated charge and is encoded as $0.00, not left blank. A charge that is a percentage or algorithm carries its derivation detail. The de-identified minimum and maximum are computed from the payer-specific negotiated charges the file itself displays.

Incompleteness is also an attestation problem, not just a data problem. Since January 1, 2025, every file affirms that all applicable standard charge information is included. A field that is absent across a whole class of services is a standing contradiction of that statement, refreshable only at the next republication.

  • 45 CFR 180.50, as cited in the CMS FAQ
