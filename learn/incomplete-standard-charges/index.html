Hospital price transparency

Incomplete standard charge fields

Last reviewed October 26, 2026

The rule names five standard charges for every item or service: the gross charge, the discounted cash price, the payer-specific negotiated charge, and the de-identified minimum and maximum negotiated charges. A file can carry thousands of rows and still be incomplete if any of the five is systematically absent.

Review each charge type against its applicable source. The required discounted-cash data element must be present. If the hospital has not established a discounted cash price, CMS says no standard charge information is required for that element. Required element presence, value applicability and source accuracy are separate checks.

Encoding details matter as much as presence. For an established $0 payer-specific negotiated charge, the June 26, 2026 CMS FAQ says not to encode a value in the payer-specific negotiated charge: dollar amount element. Explain the $0 charge in additional payer notes or additional notes, as appropriate for the chosen template. This is not the rule for the separate count of allowed amounts. For percentage or algorithm charges, follow the applicable derivation requirements. CMS says the de-identified minimum and maximum should be derived from the payer-specific negotiated charge: dollar amount element, not remittance data.

Incompleteness is also an attestation question, not just a data question. The earlier affirmation provisions began January 1, 2024; CMS lists July 1, 2024 for standardized-template affirmation-field enforcement. The modified 2026 statement says all applicable standard charge information is included and true, accurate and complete as of the file date. An omitted applicable field should be investigated and corrected in the affected disclosure; do not treat the statement as evidence that the data are complete.
